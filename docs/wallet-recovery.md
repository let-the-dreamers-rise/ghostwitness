# Wallet login and recording recovery

This is the current owner flow. There is no recovery-phone pairing, session-code entry or separate owner recovery secret. MetaMask signs a public authorization. Its private key stays inside MetaMask; it does not directly decrypt video. A trusted wallet-authorized key service unwraps the recording key and sends it encrypted to a fresh key on the recovering phone. The phone reconstructs and decrypts the media locally.

## End to end

1. Configure the same HTTPS key-service origin and Reown project ID on owner devices. On the recorder, open **Online setup and owner recovery**, choose **Connect MetaMask and sign in**, approve the connection and sign the GhostWitness authorization. The wallet address becomes the owner automatically.
2. The service supplies a stable X25519 public key for that wallet. The signed authorization binds the owner, key-service origin, public key, recorder device address and validity window. The recorder saves it in its encrypted vault. Sign in before going offline: the recorder delegation lasts 365 days; the recovery bearer token lasts one hour. Expired bearer tokens do not prevent offline capture during a valid delegation.
3. The recorder generates a fresh AES-256-GCM content key and wraps it to the service's wallet-specific public key using HPKE (X25519/HKDF-SHA256/AES-256-GCM). It signs the session header with its delegated device key. The resulting `GWO1` proof connects the wallet authorization to this exact recording and wrapped key.
4. Camera/microphone capture becomes roughly two-second H.264/AAC MP4 segments. Each segment is encrypted, split into five Reed-Solomon shards (any three distinct indices suffice), committed and signed. Nearby witnesses receive ciphertext shards, `GWO1` ownership, `GWP` segment proofs and `GWT` upload assignments. They persist these before acknowledging stored shards.
5. When suitable internet is available, each witness uploads its exact encrypted shard bytes to Bee/Swarm and verifies readback. It publishes `session`, `segment` and `upload` entities on Arkiv, owned by the original wallet. The session entity contains the public signed ownership proof; uploads contain ordinary Swarm references. Persistent jobs retry independently of the receiving screen.
6. On any new Android phone, configure the same key service plus Arkiv and a Bee read endpoint. Connect the same MetaMask account and sign in. Choose **Find my recordings**. The app queries owner-scoped Arkiv session entities, verifies the wallet and delegated recorder signatures, and lists trusted recordings newest first by date/time. Public owner tags alone cannot establish authenticity.
7. Select **Recover**. The app sends the selected proof and a fresh delivery public key to `/unlock`, using its short-lived login token. The service checks the wallet owner, recorder proof and recovery key, unwraps the content key, then HPKE-wraps it to that delivery public key. The app opens that response locally.
8. The app queries segment and upload records, verifies signed metadata and downloaded shard commitments, reconstructs segments with at least three valid distinct shards, checks available hash-chain links and decrypts them locally. It plays recovered MP4s in order and reports gaps. Plaintext and a report remain in private `files/recovered/<sid>/`.
9. Optional rewards require a separately deployed, funded pool and verifier. Receiving/ACKing a shard alone earns nothing. Only a verified uploaded contribution can be paid. The default reward chain is Base Sepolia; Arkiv metadata remains on Rabdi.

## Configuration and deployment

Create a project in the [Reown dashboard](https://dashboard.reown.com/) and copy its public 32-hex project ID into **Connection settings**. Android uses Reown Sign SDK 1.6.0, requesting Ethereum account connection and `personal_sign` only. The current integration targets MetaMask EOA accounts, not smart-contract wallets. The callback is `ghostwitness://wallet`; deep-link contents are never accepted as authentication.

Deploy the service following [key-service/README.md](../key-service/README.md). Save its canonical HTTPS origin (for example `https://keys.example.com`, with no path) on every owner device. A placeholder URL will not work. The service must be reachable for initial sign-in and recovery, but recording after setup works offline. Keep the service master and origin stable. Changing either does not migrate existing recordings.

Receiving witnesses need an HTTPS Bee API, postage batch, Rabdi RPC/access credentials if required, and a funded device writer address (shown in Connection settings). Owner recovery needs only Bee reads and Arkiv reads, plus the key service and wallet connection; it needs neither postage nor a funded writer. The default upload constraint is unmetered internet. Configure the reward verifier only after deploying/funding it; evidence uploads proceed independently of rewards.

## Trust, privacy and limits

The key-service operator can derive every wallet recovery key and decrypt recordings if it obtains their wrapped content keys and ciphertext. This is a custodial recovery design, not confidentiality against the service operator. Back up the master securely and restrict service access; losing it permanently loses recovery. Compromising it compromises recording confidentiality. Never give the service your wallet private key. The wallet signs a single-use five-minute challenge; bearer tokens expire after one hour. App sign-out clears local credentials and disconnects the wallet; already issued server tokens expire naturally.

Arkiv entities are public. Wallet addresses, delegated recorder address, signed authorization, timing, shard sizes and references are visible. The wallet signature authorizes a recorder for its validity window; anyone controlling that device's signing key can create recordings under the delegation. There is no delegation revocation UI yet. These proofs establish authorization/integrity, not that a camera depicted a truthful event.

Only newly captured wallet-authorized recordings have discovery proofs. Older sessions created with a manually supplied recovery public key are not migrated or unlockable by this service. Preserve their original key/backups and use the older recovery tooling if needed. The internal session identifier/key hash remains a verification primitive; owners do not enter it.

Interruptions preserve sealed segments that reached durable storage/witnesses. The open MP4, queued unsealed work, unavailable shards, and a tail whose metadata never uploaded cannot be recovered. Three copies of the same shard are insufficient. Android force-stop, battery restrictions or erased storage can stop pending upload work. Arkiv index entities expire after approximately 30 days unless renewed/backed up; Swarm postage/retention must also be maintained.

The service and wallet integration have local automated/build validation. Reown/MetaMask handoff, Nearby, real camera capture, live Bee/Arkiv upload and cross-phone playback still require configured infrastructure and physical-device testing. No service deployment or live end-to-end run is claimed.
